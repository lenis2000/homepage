Under mild technical assumptions on the function <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>H</mi><mn>0</mn></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>u</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo>=</mo><mi>ln</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msub><mi mathvariant="script">F</mi><mn>0</mn></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>u</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">H_0(u)=\ln(\mathcal{F}_0(u))</annotation></semantics></math></span>, we show that the limit shape <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>ω</mi><mo>∗</mo></msup><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>x</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\omega^*(x)</annotation></semantics></math></span> exists and is given by the equation <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>y</mi><mo>=</mo><msup><mi>γ</mi><mrow><mo>−</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow></msup><msub><mi>H</mi><mn>0</mn></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msup><mi mathvariant="normal">e</mi><mrow><mo>−</mo><mi>γ</mi><mi>x</mi></mrow></msup><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">y=\gamma^{-1}H_0(\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma x})</annotation></semantics></math></span>, where <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>γ</mi><mn>2</mn></msup><mo>=</mo><msubsup><mo>∫</mo><mn>0</mn><mn>1</mn></msubsup><msup><mi>u</mi><mrow><mo>−</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow></msup><msub><mi>H</mi><mn>0</mn></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>u</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mtext> </mtext><mi mathvariant="normal">d</mi><mi>u</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\gamma^2=\int_0^1 u^{-1}H_0(u)\,\mathrm{d}u</annotation></semantics></math></span>. The wide class of partition measures covered by this result includes (but is not limited to) representatives of the three meta-types of decomposable combinatorial structures --- assemblies, multisets and selections. Our method is based on the usual randomization and conditioning; to this end, a suitable local limit theorem is proved. The proofs are greatly facilitated by working with the cumulants of sums of the part counts rather than with their moments.</p>{% endraw %}
