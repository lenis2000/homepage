{% raw %}<p>The one-dimensional Brownian motion starting from the origin at time <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>t</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">t=0</annotation></semantics></math></span>, conditioned to return to the origin at time <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>t</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">t=1</annotation></semantics></math></span> and to stay positive during time interval <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>0</mn><mo>&lt;</mo><mi>t</mi><mo>&lt;</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">0 &lt; t &lt; 1</annotation></semantics></math></span>, is called the Bessel bridge with duration 1. We consider the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span>-particle system of such Bessel bridges conditioned never to collide with each other in <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>0</mn><mo>&lt;</mo><mi>t</mi><mo>&lt;</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">0 &lt; t &lt; 1</annotation></semantics></math></span>, which is the continuum limit of the vicious walk model in watermelon configuration with a wall. Distributions of maximum-values of paths attained in the time interval <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>t</mi><mo>∈</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mn>0</mn><mo separator="true">,</mo><mn>1</mn><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">t \in (0,1)</annotation></semantics></math></span> are studied to characterize the statistics of random patterns of the repulsive paths on the spatio-temporal plane. For the outermost path, the distribution function of maximum value is exactly determined for general <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span>. We show that the present <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span>-path system of noncolliding Bessel bridges is realized as the positive-eigenvalue process of the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>2</mn><mi>N</mi><mo>×</mo><mn>2</mn><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">2N \times 2N</annotation></semantics></math></span> matrix-valued Brownian bridge in the symmetry class C. Using this fact computer simulations are performed and numerical results on the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span>-dependence of the maximum-value distributions of the inner paths are reported. The present work demonstrates that the extreme-value problems of noncolliding paths are related with the random matrix theory, representation theory of symmetry, and the number theory.</p>{% endraw %}
