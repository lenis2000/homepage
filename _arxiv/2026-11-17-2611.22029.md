{% raw %}<p>Quantum measurement has opened a new frontier in nonequilibrium many-body physics, revealing striking phenomena such as measurement-induced entanglement transitions, whose experimental observation typically requires costly postselection. Quantum jump statistics offer another route to studying such measurement-induced many-body phenomena without postselection, motivating the search for universality in measurement outcomes themselves. In this Letter, we discover that fluctuation of the number of quantum jumps within a subsystem exhibits novel dynamic scaling, characterized by diffusive time scale and anomalous super-Poissonian system-size scaling that depend on the subsystem size in monitored many-body systems. For free fermions under continuous monitoring, we exactly determine the scaling exponent and the scaling function by deriving an exact finite-size solution of the Liouvillian autocorrelation function. We also analytically show that the dynamic scaling with the same scaling exponent and the scaling function even holds for interacting fermions in the limit of large measurement strength. Our work provides a novel framework to classify measurement-induced nonequilibrium many-body universality through experimentally friendly, postselection-free quantities.</p>{% endraw %}
