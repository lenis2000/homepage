{% raw %}<p>Hermite and Laguerre <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta</annotation></semantics></math></span>-ensembles are important and well studied models in random matrix theory, with the special cases <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>1</mn><mo separator="true">,</mo><mn>2</mn><mo separator="true">,</mo><mn>4</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta=1,2,4</annotation></semantics></math></span> corresponding to classical matrix ensembles. Although sharp moderate-deviation estimates have been established for the largest eigenvalues, substantially less is known for other edge eigenvalues. Even for the limiting distributions <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>T</mi><msubsup><mi>W</mi><mi>β</mi><mrow><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>k</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow></msubsup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">TW_\beta^{(k)}</annotation></semantics></math></span>, the leading constants in the tail asymptotics are not known for general <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta</annotation></semantics></math></span> and <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>k</mi><mo>≥</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">k\ge2</annotation></semantics></math></span>. In this paper, for each fixed <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>k</mi><mo>≥</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">k\ge2</annotation></semantics></math></span>, we prove matching upper and lower moderate deviation bounds for the right tail of the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>k</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">k</annotation></semantics></math></span>-th largest eigenvalue, with the optimal exponential constant <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>2</mn><mi>β</mi><mi>k</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mn>3</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">2\beta k/3</annotation></semantics></math></span>, for general <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta</annotation></semantics></math></span>. We also establish sharp lower tail estimates for the second largest eigenvalue of the Gaussian and Laguerre orthogonal ensembles, with exponential constant <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>1</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mn>24</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">1/24</annotation></semantics></math></span>, same as for the largest eigenvalue. For <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>T</mi><msubsup><mi>W</mi><mi>β</mi><mrow><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>k</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow></msubsup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">TW_\beta^{(k)}</annotation></semantics></math></span>, we obtain optimal upper and lower tail asymptotics for general <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta</annotation></semantics></math></span> and <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>k</mi><mo>≥</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">k\geq 1</annotation></semantics></math></span>. We also obtain new stochastic domination results for the edge eigenvalues. Our proofs combine variational arguments from tridiagonal matrix models, last passage percolation, stochastic comparisons of sums of edge eigenvalues, and superposition-decimation identities.</p>{% endraw %}
