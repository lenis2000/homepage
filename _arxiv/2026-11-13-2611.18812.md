{% raw %}<p>For a directed polymer in a discrete fractional Gaussian environment, we identify the scaling limit of its partition field with the renormalized Stratonovich solution of the one-dimensional parabolic Anderson model with fractional noise and flat initial condition. The underlying walk is simple and symmetric, and the temporal and spatial Hurst indices satisfy <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>H</mi><mn>0</mn></msub><mo>&gt;</mo><mfrac><mn>1</mn><mn>2</mn></mfrac></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">H_0&gt;\frac12</annotation></semantics></math></span>, <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mfrac><mn>1</mn><mn>2</mn></mfrac><mo>≤</mo><msub><mi>H</mi><mn>1</mn></msub><mo>&lt;</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\frac12\le H_1&lt;1</annotation></semantics></math></span> and <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>2</mn><msub><mi>H</mi><mn>0</mn></msub><mo>+</mo><msub><mi>H</mi><mn>1</mn></msub><mo>&lt;</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">2H_0+H_1&lt;2</annotation></semantics></math></span>. After an explicit deterministic normalization, the interpolated partition field converges in law in <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>C</mi><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">l</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">o</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">c</mi></mrow></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mo stretchy="false">[</mo><mn>0</mn><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">∞</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo>×</mo><mi mathvariant="double-struck">R</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">C_{\rm loc}([0,\infty)\times\R)</annotation></semantics></math></span>, with convergence of all finite moments of its supremum norm on compacts. We construct the limiting field directly by smoothing the noise in the equation and subtracting the corresponding stationary counterterm. The classical solutions converge in <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>L</mi><mi>p</mi></msup><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">Ω</mi><mo separator="true">;</mo><mi>C</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi mathvariant="script">K</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">L^p(\Omega;C(\mathcal K))</annotation></semantics></math></span> for every <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>1</mn><mo>≤</mo><mi>p</mi><mo>&lt;</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">∞</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">1\le p&lt;\infty</annotation></semantics></math></span> and every compact space-time rectangle <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi mathvariant="script">K</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\mathcal K</annotation></semantics></math></span>, including those that meet the initial line <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>t</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">t=0</annotation></semantics></math></span>. For <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>2</mn><msub><mi>H</mi><mn>0</mn></msub><mo>+</mo><msub><mi>H</mi><mn>1</mn></msub><mo>&gt;</mo><mfrac><mn>5</mn><mn>3</mn></mfrac></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">2H_0+H_1&gt;\frac53</annotation></semantics></math></span>, the limit agrees with the solution that Chen, Ouyang, Tindel and the author constructed using regularity structures. The construction also covers the new range <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mfrac><mn>3</mn><mn>2</mn></mfrac><mo>&lt;</mo><mn>2</mn><msub><mi>H</mi><mn>0</mn></msub><mo>+</mo><msub><mi>H</mi><mn>1</mn></msub><mo>≤</mo><mfrac><mn>5</mn><mn>3</mn></mfrac></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\frac32&lt;2H_0+H_1\le\frac53</annotation></semantics></math></span>. The proofs rely on Feynman-Kac representations and on estimates and invariance principles for weighted self- and mutual intersections of random walks and Brownian motions, together with uniform increment estimates and Gaussian hypercontractivity.</p>{% endraw %}
