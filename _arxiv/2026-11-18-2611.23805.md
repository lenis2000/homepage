{% raw %}<p>In this paper we consider the Toda lattice under random, spatially inhomogeneous initial data, meaning that its initial momenta and interparticle spacings are independent and their laws may slowly vary in space. When the density is strictly positive (but sufficiently small), we identify the model's hydrodynamic limit under Euler scaling. More precisely, we prove that spatial averages of each of the model's local conserved quantities converge to moments of a density <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>m</mi><mi>τ</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>λ</mi><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi>q</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">m_\tau(\lambda,q)</annotation></semantics></math></span> solving the generalized hydrodynamic equation predicted by Doyon arXiv:1902.07624 and Spohn arXiv:1902.07751. To do so, we justify the notion that the Toda lattice can be thought of as a dense collection of many "quasiparticles" that interact through scattering. As in Aggarwal arXiv:2503.08018, we define these quasiparticles through spectral properties of the Toda Lax matrix, but in our inhomogeneous setting we must now show that the eigenvectors of this Lax matrix remain localized throughout the dynamics. The core of our work is to show that, with high probability, the quasiparticle trajectories converge to the characteristic curves of the generalized hydrodynamic equation.</p>{% endraw %}
