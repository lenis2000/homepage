{% raw %}<p>The two-dimensional one-component plasma, <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span> logarithmically interacting point charges in a neutralizing background, is solved exactly at coupling <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">Γ</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\Gamma=2</annotation></semantics></math></span> on the truncated Beltrami pseudosphere, the tractrix-generated surface of constant curvature <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>−</mo><mn>1</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><msup><mi>a</mi><mn>2</mn></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">-1/a^2</annotation></semantics></math></span>, bounded by its rim and by a second parallel at meridian parameter <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>u</mi><mo>=</mo><msub><mi>u</mi><mi>max</mi><mo>⁡</mo></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">u=u_{\max}</annotation></semantics></math></span>. A conformal map takes the surface onto a flat cylinder, where the interaction is the flat-cylinder potential and the curvature enters only through the area element; the partition function is a product of incomplete gamma functions, the density is explicit and all correlations are determinantal. The thermodynamic limit is taken at fixed background density, with <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>u</mi><mi>max</mi><mo>⁡</mo></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">u_{\max}</annotation></semantics></math></span> fixed and <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>a</mi><mo>∝</mo><msqrt><mi>N</mi></msqrt></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">a\propto\sqrt{N}</annotation></semantics></math></span>, as on the sphere; the logarithmic interaction having no intrinsic length, this is equivalent to letting <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span> grow on a fixed surface. The free energy is obtained through the constant term, all coefficients in closed form: a bulk term proportional to <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span>, the flat hard-wall surface tension times the total boundary length, and a shape-dependent constant, with no term in <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>ln</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\ln N</annotation></semantics></math></span>. This is the conformal-anomaly prediction for Euler characteristic zero, tested here with integrated Gaussian and boundary curvatures separately nonzero and cancelling; the model being a cylinder plasma with a specific axial potential, the expansion coincides term by term with the general result of Shen and Forrester, whose universal constants are the quadratures of the two boundary layers. At both walls the density tends to the flat contact value <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>n</mi><mi>b</mi></msub><mi>ln</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n_b\ln 2</annotation></semantics></math></span>, from opposite sides, although their circumferences differ by a factor <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>cosh</mi><mo>⁡</mo><msub><mi>u</mi><mi>max</mi><mo>⁡</mo></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\cosh u_{\max}</annotation></semantics></math></span>. Asymptotics are checked against the exact solution up to <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>4096</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N=4096</annotation></semantics></math></span>, and Monte Carlo simulations at <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">Γ</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\Gamma=1</annotation></semantics></math></span>, 4 and 6, validated on the exact <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">Γ</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\Gamma=2</annotation></semantics></math></span> density, show how both boundary layers evolve with the coupling.</p>{% endraw %}
