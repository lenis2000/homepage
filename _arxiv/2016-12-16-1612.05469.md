{% raw %}<p>Invariant ensembles of random matrices are characterized by the distribution of their eigenvalues <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo stretchy="false">{</mo><msub><mi>λ</mi><mn>1</mn></msub><mo separator="true">,</mo><mo>⋯</mo><mtext> </mtext><mo separator="true">,</mo><msub><mi>λ</mi><mi>N</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">}</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\{\lambda_1,\cdots,\lambda_N\}</annotation></semantics></math></span>. We study the distribution of truncated linear statistics of the form <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mover accent="true"><mi>L</mi><mo>~</mo></mover><mo>=</mo><msubsup><mo>∑</mo><mrow><mi>i</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><mi>p</mi></msubsup><mi>f</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msub><mi>λ</mi><mi>i</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\tilde{L}=\sum_{i=1}^p f(\lambda_i)</annotation></semantics></math></span> with <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>p</mi><mo>&lt;</mo><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">p&lt;N</annotation></semantics></math></span>. This problem has been considered by us in a previous paper when the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>p</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">p</annotation></semantics></math></span> eigenvalues are further constrained to be the largest ones (or the smallest). In this second paper we consider the same problem without this restriction which leads to a rather different analysis. We introduce a new ensemble which is related, but not equivalent, to the "thinned ensembles" introduced by Bohigas and Pato. This question is motivated by the study of partial sums of proper time delays in chaotic quantum dots, which are characteristic times of the scattering process. Using the Coulomb gas technique, we derive the large deviation function for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mover accent="true"><mi>L</mi><mo>~</mo></mover></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\tilde{L}</annotation></semantics></math></span>. Large deviations of linear statistics <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>L</mi><mo>=</mo><msubsup><mo>∑</mo><mrow><mi>i</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><mi>N</mi></msubsup><mi>f</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msub><mi>λ</mi><mi>i</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">L=\sum_{i=1}^N f(\lambda_i)</annotation></semantics></math></span> are usually dominated by the energy of the Coulomb gas, which scales as <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>∼</mo><msup><mi>N</mi><mn>2</mn></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\sim N^2</annotation></semantics></math></span>, implying that the relative fluctuations are of order <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>1</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">1/N</annotation></semantics></math></span>. For the truncated linear statistics considered here, there is a whole region (including the typical fluctuations region), where the energy of the Coulomb gas is frozen and the large deviation function is purely controlled by an entropic effect. Because the entropy scales as <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>∼</mo><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\sim N</annotation></semantics></math></span>, the relative fluctuations are of order <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>1</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><msqrt><mi>N</mi></msqrt></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">1/\sqrt{N}</annotation></semantics></math></span>. Our analysis relies on the mapping on a problem of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>p</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">p</annotation></semantics></math></span> fictitious non-interacting fermions in <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span> energy levels, which can exhibit both positive and negative effective (absolute) temperatures. We determine the large deviation function characterizing the distribution of the truncated linear statistics, and show that, for the case considered here (<span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>f</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>λ</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo>=</mo><mn>1</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mi>λ</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">f(\lambda)=1/\lambda</annotation></semantics></math></span>), the corresponding phase diagram is separated in three different phases.</p>{% endraw %}
