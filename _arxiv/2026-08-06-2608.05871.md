{% raw %}<p>We provide limit theory for the trace of the squared sample correlation matrix <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi mathvariant="bold">R</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\mathbf R</annotation></semantics></math></span>, constructed from <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n</annotation></semantics></math></span> observations of a <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>p</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">p</annotation></semantics></math></span>-dimensional random vector with iid components. If the entries have finite fourth moment and <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>p</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">p</annotation></semantics></math></span> and <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n</annotation></semantics></math></span> grow proportionally, it is known that <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">tr</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msup><mi mathvariant="bold">R</mi><mn>2</mn></msup><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\operatorname{tr}({\mathbf R}^2)</annotation></semantics></math></span> satisfies a central limit theorem (CLT) and the centering and scaling sequences are universal in the sense that they do not depend on the entry distribution. Under symmetry and regular variation assumption with index <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>α</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\alpha</annotation></semantics></math></span> and any growth rate of the dimension, we prove that the universal CLT remains valid for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>α</mi><mo>&gt;</mo><mn>3</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\alpha&gt;3</annotation></semantics></math></span>. For <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>α</mi><mo>&lt;</mo><mn>3</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\alpha&lt;3</annotation></semantics></math></span>, we identify a critical dimension growth at which the fluctuations of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">tr</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msup><mi mathvariant="bold">R</mi><mn>2</mn></msup><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\operatorname{tr}({\mathbf R}^2)</annotation></semantics></math></span> become non-Gaussian. Moreover, if the dimension <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>p</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">p</annotation></semantics></math></span> grows faster and <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>α</mi><mo>≤</mo><mn>3</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\alpha \le 3</annotation></semantics></math></span> we establish a non-universal CLT with norming sequences depending on the value of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>α</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\alpha</annotation></semantics></math></span>. Our findings are illustrated in a simulation study.</p>{% endraw %}
