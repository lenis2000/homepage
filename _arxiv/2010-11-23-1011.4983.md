{% raw %}<p>Random Hermitian matrices are used to model complex systems without time-reversal invariance. Adding an external source to the model can have the effect of shifting some of the matrix eigenvalues, which corresponds to shifting some of the energy levels of the physical system. We consider the case when the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi><mo>×</mo><mi>n</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n\times n</annotation></semantics></math></span> external source matrix has two distinct real eigenvalues: <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>a</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">a</annotation></semantics></math></span> with multiplicity <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>r</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">r</annotation></semantics></math></span> and zero with multiplicity <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi><mo>−</mo><mi>r</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n-r</annotation></semantics></math></span>. For a Gaussian potential, it was shown by P\'ech\'e \cite{Peche:2006} that when <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>r</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">r</annotation></semantics></math></span> is fixed or grows sufficiently slowly with <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n</annotation></semantics></math></span> (a small-rank source), <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>r</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">r</annotation></semantics></math></span> eigenvalues are expected to exit the main bulk for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">∣</mi><mi>a</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">∣</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">|a|</annotation></semantics></math></span> large enough. Furthermore, at the critical value of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>a</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">a</annotation></semantics></math></span> when the outliers are at the edge of a band, the eigenvalues at the edge are described by the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>r</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">r</annotation></semantics></math></span>-Airy kernel. We establish the universality of the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>r</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">r</annotation></semantics></math></span>-Airy kernel for a general class of analytic potentials for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>r</mi><mo>=</mo><mi mathvariant="script">O</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msup><mi>n</mi><mi>γ</mi></msup><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">r=\mathcal{O}(n^\gamma)</annotation></semantics></math></span> for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>0</mn><mo>≤</mo><mi>γ</mi><mo>&lt;</mo><mn>1</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mn>12</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">0\leq\gamma&lt;1/12</annotation></semantics></math></span>.</p>{% endraw %}
