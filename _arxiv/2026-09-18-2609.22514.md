{% raw %}<p>We define a natural orientation on a spanning-tree-decorated planar map whereby, roughly speaking, each directed edge in the map is oriented to match the direction of the contour exploration of the spanning tree. We study directed distances (lengths of shortest directed paths) with respect to this orientation. We construct the Busemann function which measures directed distances to <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">∞</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\infty</annotation></semantics></math></span> along a natural interface in the uniform infinite spanning-tree-decorated map. We show that this Busemann function, re-scaled appropriately, converges in law to a <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>3</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">3/2</annotation></semantics></math></span>-stable Lévy process. We also show that in a uniform spanning-tree-decorated map with <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n</annotation></semantics></math></span> edges, directed distances are typically of order <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>n</mi><mrow><mn>1</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mn>3</mn></mrow></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n^{1/3}</annotation></semantics></math></span>. Using a strong coupling argument, we deduce analogous statements for directed distances in other random planar maps in the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msqrt><mn>2</mn></msqrt></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\sqrt 2</annotation></semantics></math></span>-Liouville quantum gravity (LQG) universality class, including uniform meandric systems and mated-CRT maps for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>γ</mi><mo>=</mo><msqrt><mn>2</mn></msqrt></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\gamma=\sqrt 2</annotation></semantics></math></span>. These results give the scaling dimension for a hypothetical directed version of the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msqrt><mn>2</mn></msqrt></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\sqrt 2</annotation></semantics></math></span>-LQG metric. Our proof strategy is inspired by work of Borga and Gwynne (2025) on directed distances in bipolar-oriented triangulations.</p>{% endraw %}
