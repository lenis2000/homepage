{% raw %}<p>A hard edge of a Hermitian random matrix occurs when the eigenvalue density is strictly zero on one side of the edge, and nonzero on the other. Another possibility is that the eigenvalues occur in <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>±</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\pm</annotation></semantics></math></span> pairs, which creates a hard edge at the origin for the positive eigenvalues. Both review material and new results are presented for random matrix ensembles exhibiting a hard edge. The review material has two focuses: one is on applications of the random matrix hard edge, while the other is on exact eigenvalue probability density functions. The new results relate to the large <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span> expansion of correlation functions and conditioned gap probability generating functions with hard edge scaling for the Jacobi ensemble. For all even <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta</annotation></semantics></math></span> we are able to show that these quantities admit expansions in inverse even powers of the variable <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msubsup><mi>N</mi><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">h</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">J</mi></mrow><mo mathvariant="normal" lspace="0em" rspace="0em">′</mo></msubsup><mo>:</mo><mo>=</mo><mi>N</mi><mo>−</mo><mn>1</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mn>2</mn><mo>+</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>a</mi><mo>+</mo><mi>b</mi><mo>+</mo><mn>1</mn><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mi>β</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N_{\rm hJ}&#x27;:= N - 1/2 + (a + b + 1)/\beta</annotation></semantics></math></span>. For the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta=2</annotation></semantics></math></span> Jacobi unitary ensemble, we can relate the leading (next leading) correction to the limit law to a particular second order (fourth order) differential operator applied to the latter. The analogous second order differential operator is found for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>1</mn><mo separator="true">,</mo><mn>4</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta=1,4</annotation></semantics></math></span>, leading then to its conjectured form for all <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi><mo>&gt;</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta&gt;0</annotation></semantics></math></span>.</p>{% endraw %}
