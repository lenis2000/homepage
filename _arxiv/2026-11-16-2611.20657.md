{% raw %}<p>Let <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>P</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">P</annotation></semantics></math></span> be a noncommutative polynomial of arbitrary degree. We prove that the empirical spectral distribution of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>P</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">P</annotation></semantics></math></span> evaluated at independent Wigner matrices converges weakly in probability to its Brown measure limit. The same conclusion holds for arbitrary <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>∗</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">*</annotation></semantics></math></span>-polynomials in independent iid matrices and their adjoints. The entry laws have bounded real or joint planar densities and a finite <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mn>2</mn><mo>+</mo><mi>ε</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">(2+\varepsilon)</annotation></semantics></math></span> moment; only second moments are required on the Wigner diagonal. The real and imaginary parts of an entry may be dependent, and its pseudovariance need not vanish. The proof develops the coefficient-algebra approach of the author's earlier work on matrix polynomials with Gaussian and non-Gaussian iid inputs \cite{Han}. The main step is a polynomial lower-tail estimate, under only second moments, for the least singular value of a tensor pencil with arbitrary fixed matrix coefficients. We use a logarithmic determinant method: a real-block small-ball argument shows that logarithmic derivatives of the determinant control the inverse pencil. Relative polynomial anti-concentration then gives the lower-tail bound. We establish convergence of logarithmic potentials by Hermitian resolvent comparison and rank truncation. This extends the recent Brown measure convergence theorem of Collins, Kitagawa, Miyagawa, Yuan and Zhong \cite{Collins} for GUE polynomials to non-Gaussian Wigner inputs.</p>{% endraw %}
