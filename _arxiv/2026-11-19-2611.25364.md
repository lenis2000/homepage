{% raw %}<p>We prove dynamical edge universality for three families of interacting particle systems for every <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi><mo>&gt;</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta&gt;0</annotation></semantics></math></span>: <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta</annotation></semantics></math></span>-Dyson Brownian motion with an analytic potential, weighted <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta</annotation></semantics></math></span>-corners processes, and a class of discrete-time Markov chains. Our results apply to general non-equilibrium initial data and show that the Airy<span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mrow></mrow><mi>β</mi></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">_\beta</annotation></semantics></math></span> line ensemble emerges at positive times whenever the limiting density has a regular square-root edge. More precisely, after centering at the moving deterministic edge and rescaling space and time on the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>n</mi><mrow><mo>−</mo><mn>2</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mn>3</mn></mrow></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n^{-2/3}</annotation></semantics></math></span> and <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>n</mi><mrow><mo>−</mo><mn>1</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mn>3</mn></mrow></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n^{-1/3}</annotation></semantics></math></span> scales, respectively, the trajectories of the top <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>k</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">k</annotation></semantics></math></span> particles converge jointly in distribution to the top <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>k</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">k</annotation></semantics></math></span> curves of the Airy<span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mrow></mrow><mi>β</mi></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">_\beta</annotation></semantics></math></span> line ensemble, for every fixed <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>k</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">k</annotation></semantics></math></span>, in the topology of uniform convergence on compact time intervals. The proof combines dynamical loop equations with characteristic-flow estimates to establish local laws near the moving edge and upper bounds on the extremal particle. We then identify the stochastic evolution of every subsequential limit of the rescaled Stieltjes transform and establish convergence through the pole-evolution characterization of the Airy<span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mrow></mrow><mi>β</mi></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">_\beta</annotation></semantics></math></span> line ensemble.</p>{% endraw %}
