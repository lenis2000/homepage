{% raw %}<p>Random Hermitian matrices with a source term arise, for instance, in the study of non-intersecting Brownian walkers \cite{Adler:2009a, Daems:2007} and sample covariance matrices \cite{Baik:2005}. We consider the case when the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi><mo>×</mo><mi>n</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n\times n</annotation></semantics></math></span> external source matrix has two distinct real eigenvalues: <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>a</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">a</annotation></semantics></math></span> with multiplicity <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>r</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">r</annotation></semantics></math></span> and zero with multiplicity <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi><mo>−</mo><mi>r</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n-r</annotation></semantics></math></span>. The source is small in the sense that <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>r</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">r</annotation></semantics></math></span> is finite or <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>r</mi><mo>=</mo><mi mathvariant="script">O</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msup><mi>n</mi><mi>γ</mi></msup><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">r=\mathcal O(n^\gamma)</annotation></semantics></math></span>, for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>0</mn><mo>&lt;</mo><mi>γ</mi><mo>&lt;</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">0&lt; \gamma&lt;1</annotation></semantics></math></span>. For a Gaussian potential, P\'ech\'e \cite{Peche:2006} showed that for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">∣</mi><mi>a</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">∣</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">|a|</annotation></semantics></math></span> sufficiently small (the subcritical regime) the external source has no leading-order effect on the eigenvalues, while for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">∣</mi><mi>a</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">∣</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">|a|</annotation></semantics></math></span> sufficiently large (the supercritical regime) <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>r</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">r</annotation></semantics></math></span> eigenvalues exit the bulk of the spectrum and behave as the eigenvalues of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>r</mi><mo>×</mo><mi>r</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">r\times r</annotation></semantics></math></span> Gaussian unitary ensemble (GUE). We establish the universality of these results for a general class of analytic potentials in the supercritical and subcritical regimes.</p>{% endraw %}
