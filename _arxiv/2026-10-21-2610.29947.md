{% raw %}<p>We study joint moments of characteristic polynomials and their second derivatives for Haar-distributed matrices from the unitary symplectic and orthogonal groups. We establish their large-<span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span> asymptotics throughout the full admissible range of the moment parameters and identify the limiting coefficients probabilistically in terms of the first inverse power sum of the Bessel point process. This extends the corresponding results of Assiotis, Gunes, Keating, and Wei [Proc. Lond. Math. Soc., 132 (2026), e70136] to the full admissible parameter range. Throughout this full range, we also express these limits in terms of a distinguished solution of the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>σ</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\sigma</annotation></semantics></math></span>-Painlevé III' equation. Our approach reduces the problem to singular inverse-power statistics of the Jacobi unitary ensemble and combines their finite-<span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span> <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>σ</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\sigma</annotation></semantics></math></span>-Painlevé V representation with a refined Riemann-Hilbert analysis yielding precise small-<span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>x</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">x</annotation></semantics></math></span> asymptotics and uniform derivative estimates for the finite-<span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span> correction term. A key ingredient in the probabilistic identification is a kernel-based proof that the rescaled first inverse power sum of the Jacobi ensemble converges in distribution to its Bessel point process counterpart.</p>{% endraw %}
