We describe the support of this process by means of a polymatroid which is described in terms of the circular and bicircular matroids. We also explain how the DLSF relates to the computation of currents in an electrical network in the presence of a gauge field. The partition function of the DLSF is shown to be the determinant of the twisted Laplacian, for which we also give a matrix-tree type theorem that computes its characteristic polynomial. We initiate the study of the twisted Laplacian determinant as a functional on the space of connections, and give a first order expansion of it in a neighborhood of the trivial connection, for which we provide a probabilistic interpretation. We extend the construction to infinite graphs, exhibiting the analogs of the classical wired and free distributions on spanning forests. We give a sufficient geometric condition on the local spectral gap for the existence of a Poincaré inequality as well as the uniqueness of the infinite volume measure, and the exponential decay of correlations. We show that on any recurrent graph, the wired and free DLSF coincide. We construct examples where they do not, for example, unlike in the classical case, on <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi mathvariant="double-struck">Z</mi><mi>d</mi></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\mathbb Z^d</annotation></semantics></math></span> for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>d</mi><mo>≥</mo><mn>3</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">d\geq 3</annotation></semantics></math></span>.</p>{% endraw %}
