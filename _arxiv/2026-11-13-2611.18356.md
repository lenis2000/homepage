{% raw %}<p>We study squared Bessel particle systems with repulsion parameter <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>κ</mi><mo>&gt;</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\kappa&gt;0</annotation></semantics></math></span> and dimension parameter <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>δ</mi><mo>∈</mo><mi mathvariant="double-struck">R</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\delta \in\mathbb R</annotation></semantics></math></span>. We prove that the singular equation has a unique global strong solution for every initial state in the closed Weyl chamber, including states with coinciding particles. The solutions have locally integrable interaction terms and spend zero Lebesgue time at pairwise collisions. We also consider the equation in which the interaction terms are set to zero at collisions. At certain integer values of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>δ</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\delta</annotation></semantics></math></span>, this equation admits infinitely many solution laws from suitable initial configurations. In these solutions, a block of particles can remain at zero for any prescribed time before being released. For positive initial states, we determine when zero is reached and when particles enter the negative half-line. From distinct initial positions, collisions occur almost surely for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>0</mn><mo>&lt;</mo><mi>κ</mi><mo>&lt;</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">0&lt;\kappa&lt;1</annotation></semantics></math></span> and do not occur for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>κ</mi><mo>≥</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\kappa \ge1</annotation></semantics></math></span>. Collisions of three or more particles are excluded away from zero. We give an example of a triple collision at zero. We obtain two-sided bounds for the tails of the first-zero and first-collision times. The bounds give matching powers for the first-zero time when it is finite, and for the first-collision time in nonnegative systems when <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>0</mn><mo>&lt;</mo><mi>κ</mi><mo>&lt;</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">0&lt;\kappa&lt;1</annotation></semantics></math></span>.</p>{% endraw %}
