{% raw %}<p>We consider <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span> classical particles interacting via the Coulomb potential in spatial dimension <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>d</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">d</annotation></semantics></math></span> and in the presence of an external trap, at equilibrium at inverse temperature <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta</annotation></semantics></math></span>. In the large <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span> limit, the particles are confined within a droplet of finite size. We study smooth linear statistics, i.e. the fluctuations of sums of the form <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi mathvariant="script">L</mi><mi>N</mi></msub><mo>=</mo><msubsup><mo>∑</mo><mrow><mi>i</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><mi>N</mi></msubsup><mi>f</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msub><mi mathvariant="bold">x</mi><mi>i</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\cal L}_N = \sum_{i=1}^N f({\bf x}_i)</annotation></semantics></math></span>, where <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi mathvariant="bold">x</mi><mi>i</mi></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\bf x}_i</annotation></semantics></math></span>'s are the positions of the particles and where <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>f</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msub><mi mathvariant="bold">x</mi><mi>i</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">f({\bf x}_i)</annotation></semantics></math></span> is a sufficiently regular function. There exists at present standard results for the first and second moments of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi mathvariant="script">L</mi><mi>N</mi></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\cal L}_N</annotation></semantics></math></span> in the large <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span> limit, as well as associated Central Limit Theorems in general dimension and for a wide class of confining potentials. Here we obtain explicit expressions for the higher order cumulants of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi mathvariant="script">L</mi><mi>N</mi></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\cal L}_N</annotation></semantics></math></span> at large <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span>, when the function <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>f</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi mathvariant="bold">x</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo>=</mo><mi>f</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">∣</mi><mi mathvariant="bold">x</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">∣</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">f({\bf x})=f(|{\bf x}|)</annotation></semantics></math></span> and the confining potential are both rotationnally invariant. A remarkable feature of our results is that these higher cumulants depend only on the value of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>f</mi><mo mathvariant="normal" lspace="0em" rspace="0em">′</mo></msup><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">∣</mi><mi mathvariant="bold">x</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">∣</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">f&#x27;(|{\bf x}|)</annotation></semantics></math></span> and its higher order derivatives evaluated exactly at the boundary of the droplet, which in this case is a <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>d</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">d</annotation></semantics></math></span>-dimensional sphere. In the particular two-dimensional case <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>d</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">d=2</annotation></semantics></math></span> at the special value <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta=2</annotation></semantics></math></span>, a connection to the Ginibre ensemble allows us to derive these results in an alternative way using the tools of determinantal point processes. Finally we also obtain the large deviation form of the full probability distribution function of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi mathvariant="script">L</mi><mi>N</mi></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\cal L}_N</annotation></semantics></math></span>.</p>{% endraw %}
