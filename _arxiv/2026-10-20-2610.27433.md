{% raw %}<p>In directed last-passage percolation with i.i.d. exponential vertex weights, the coalescence time <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">Coal</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>a</mi><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi>b</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\operatorname{Coal}(a,b)</annotation></semantics></math></span> of two semi-infinite geodesics started from vertices <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>a</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">a</annotation></semantics></math></span> and <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>b</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">b</annotation></semantics></math></span> in a fixed direction has a heavy tail with (KPZ) exponent <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>2</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mn>3</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">2/3</annotation></semantics></math></span>, and hence infinite expectation. We study instead the first coalescence among triples of semi-infinite geodesics in first- and last-passage percolation models (FPP/LPP) on <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi mathvariant="double-struck">Z</mi><mn>2</mn></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\mathbb{Z}^2</annotation></semantics></math></span> that arise from stationary compatible families, such as Busemann geodesics. For directed FPP and LPP with arbitrary weights, we prove that <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="block"><semantics><mrow><mi mathvariant="double-struck">E</mi><mo fence="true" stretchy="true" minsize="1.2em" maxsize="1.2em">[</mo><mi>min</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mo stretchy="false">{</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">Coal</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>a</mi><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi>a</mi><mo>−</mo><msub><mi>e</mi><mn>1</mn></msub><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">Coal</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>a</mi><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi>a</mi><mo>−</mo><msub><mi>e</mi><mn>2</mn></msub><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo stretchy="false">}</mo><mo fence="true" stretchy="true" minsize="1.2em" maxsize="1.2em">]</mo><mo>&lt;</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">∞</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">.</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\mathbb{E}\bigl[\min\{\operatorname{Coal}(a,a-e_1),\operatorname{Coal}(a,a-e_2)\}\bigr]&lt;\infty.</annotation></semantics></math></span> We also consider FPP and LPP models in which antidiagonal motion is allowed, and prove that for every integer <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>k</mi><mo>&gt;</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">k&gt;0</annotation></semantics></math></span>, \[\mathbb{E}\bigl[\min\{\operatorname{Coal}(a,a-k(e_1-e_2)),\operatorname{Coal}(a,a+k(e_1-e_2))\}\bigr]<\infty.\] These results support the heuristic prediction that adjacent coalescence times are negatively associated.</p>{% endraw %}
