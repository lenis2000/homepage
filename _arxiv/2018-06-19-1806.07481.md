{% raw %}<p>We consider the spatial quantum and thermal fluctuations of non-interacting Fermi gases of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span> particles confined in <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>d</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">d</annotation></semantics></math></span>-dimensional non-smooth potentials. We first present a thorough study of the spherically symmetric pure hard-box potential, with vanishing potential inside the box, both at <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>T</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">T=0</annotation></semantics></math></span> and <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>T</mi><mo>&gt;</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">T&gt;0</annotation></semantics></math></span>. We find that the correlations near the wall are described by a "hard edge" kernel, which depend both on <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>d</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">d</annotation></semantics></math></span> and <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>T</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">T</annotation></semantics></math></span>, and which is different from the "soft edge" Airy kernel, and its higher <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>d</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">d</annotation></semantics></math></span> generalizations, found for smooth potentials. We extend these results to the case where the potential is non-uniform inside the box, and find that there exists a family of kernels which interpolate between the above "hard edge" kernel and the "soft edge" kernels. Finally, we consider one-dimensional singular potentials of the form <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>V</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>x</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo>∼</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">∣</mi><mi>x</mi><msup><mi mathvariant="normal">∣</mi><mrow><mo>−</mo><mi>γ</mi></mrow></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">V(x)\sim |x|^{-\gamma}</annotation></semantics></math></span> with <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>γ</mi><mo>&gt;</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\gamma&gt;0</annotation></semantics></math></span>. We show that the correlations close to the singularity at <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>x</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">x=0</annotation></semantics></math></span> are described by this "hard edge" kernel for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>1</mn><mo>≤</mo><mi>γ</mi><mo>&lt;</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">1\leq\gamma&lt;2</annotation></semantics></math></span> while they are described by a broader family of "hard edge" kernels known as the Bessel kernel for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>γ</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\gamma=2</annotation></semantics></math></span> and, finally by the Airy kernel for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>γ</mi><mo>&gt;</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\gamma&gt;2</annotation></semantics></math></span>. These one-dimensional kernels also appear in random matrix theory, and we provide here the mapping between the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>1</mn><mi>d</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">1d</annotation></semantics></math></span> fermion models and the corresponding random matrix ensembles. Part of these results were announced in a recent Letter, EPL 120, 10006 (2017).</p>{% endraw %}
