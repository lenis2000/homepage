{% raw %}<p>Forty years after it was written down, the Kardar--Parisi--Zhang (KPZ) equation is the paradigm of scale invariance far from equilibrium. In one dimension it has been solved exactly and confirmed by precision experiments, and its scaling has now been observed in two-dimensional polariton condensates. Above one dimension, however, not a single exponent is known exactly, and even the existence of an upper critical dimension is disputed. In this Perspective we argue that firm statements need not wait for exact solutions. Treating the nonlinearity as an effective noise that acts on a fractal subset of the substrate leads to bounds on the roughness exponent, <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>1</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>d</mi><mo>+</mo><mn>1</mn><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo>≤</mo><mi>α</mi><mo>≤</mo><mn>1</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mi>d</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">1/(d+1)\le\alpha \le 1/d</annotation></semantics></math></span> in <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>d</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">d</annotation></semantics></math></span> dimensions. We examine the assumptions behind these bounds, confront them with four decades of theory, simulations and the new experiments, and identify the tests---foremost in three and more dimensions---that would establish or refute them.</p>{% endraw %}
