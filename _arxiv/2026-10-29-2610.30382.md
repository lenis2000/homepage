{% raw %}<p>We investigate the longest increasing subsequences (LIS) of random permutations distributed under the Luce model with weights <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msubsup><mi>θ</mi><mn>1</mn><mrow><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>n</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow></msubsup><mo>≥</mo><mo>…</mo><mo>≥</mo><msubsup><mi>θ</mi><mi>n</mi><mrow><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>n</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow></msubsup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\theta_1^{(n)} \ge \ldots \ge \theta_n^{(n)}</annotation></semantics></math></span>. Our first main result provides a general formula for the growth rate of the LIS. For example, we get that the LIS of Luce permutations with Sukhatme weights <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msubsup><mi>θ</mi><mi>k</mi><mrow><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>n</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow></msubsup><mo>=</mo><mi>n</mi><mo>−</mo><mi>k</mi><mo>+</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\theta_k^{(n)} = n-k+1</annotation></semantics></math></span> grows like <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msqrt><mi>n</mi></msqrt></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\sqrt{n}</annotation></semantics></math></span>. We also give simple conditions for the LIS to be of order <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>n</mi><mi>β</mi></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n^\beta</annotation></semantics></math></span>, for any given <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi><mo>∈</mo><mo stretchy="false">[</mo><mn>1</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mn>2</mn><mo separator="true">,</mo><mn>1</mn><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta \in[1/2,1)</annotation></semantics></math></span>. Our second main result states that, if the weights are of the form <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msubsup><mi>θ</mi><mi>k</mi><mrow><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>n</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow></msubsup><mo>=</mo><mi>f</mi><mo fence="false" stretchy="true" minsize="1.2em" maxsize="1.2em">(</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>k</mi><mo>−</mo><mn>1</mn><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mi>n</mi><mo fence="false" stretchy="true" minsize="1.2em" maxsize="1.2em">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\theta_k^{(n)} = f\big( (k-1)/n \big)</annotation></semantics></math></span> for a sufficiently regular function <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>f</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">f</annotation></semantics></math></span>, then the LIS divided by <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>2</mn><msqrt><mi>n</mi></msqrt></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">2\sqrt{n}</annotation></semantics></math></span> converges in probability to a constant <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>K</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>f</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">K(f)</annotation></semantics></math></span> described by a variational problem. Throughout, we make extensive use of coupling techniques; in particular, to prove our second main result, we argue that Luce random permutations behave similarly to random permutations sampled from the ``Luce permuton''.</p>{% endraw %}
