We assume that the variances satisfy the normalization condition <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mo>∑</mo><mi>i</mi></msub><msubsup><mi>σ</mi><mrow><mi>i</mi><mi>j</mi></mrow><mn>2</mn></msubsup><mo>=</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\sum_{i} \sigma^2_{ij} = 1</annotation></semantics></math></span> for all <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>j</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">j</annotation></semantics></math></span> and that there is a positive constant <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>c</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">c</annotation></semantics></math></span> such that <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>c</mi><mo>≤</mo><mi>N</mi><msubsup><mi>σ</mi><mrow><mi>i</mi><mi>j</mi></mrow><mn>2</mn></msubsup><mo>≤</mo><msup><mi>c</mi><mrow><mo>−</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">c\le N \sigma_{ij}^2 \le c^{-1}</annotation></semantics></math></span>. We further assume that the probability distributions <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>ν</mi><mrow><mi>i</mi><mi>j</mi></mrow></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\nu_{ij}</annotation></semantics></math></span> have a uniform subexponential decay. We prove that the Stieltjes transform of the empirical eigenvalue distribution of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>H</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">H</annotation></semantics></math></span> is given by the Wigner semicircle law uniformly up to the edges of the spectrum with an error of order <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>N</mi><mi>η</mi><msup><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mrow><mo>−</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex"> (N \eta)^{-1}</annotation></semantics></math></span> where <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>η</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\eta</annotation></semantics></math></span> is the imaginary part of the spectral parameter in the Stieltjes transform. There are three corollaries to this strong local semicircle law: (1) Rigidity of eigenvalues: If <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>γ</mi><mi>j</mi></msub><mo>=</mo><msub><mi>γ</mi><mrow><mi>j</mi><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi>N</mi></mrow></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\gamma_j =\gamma_{j,N}</annotation></semantics></math></span> denotes the {\it classical location} of the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>j</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">j</annotation></semantics></math></span>-th eigenvalue under the semicircle law ordered in increasing order, then the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>j</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">j</annotation></semantics></math></span>-th eigenvalue <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>λ</mi><mi>j</mi></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\lambda_j</annotation></semantics></math></span> is close to <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>γ</mi><mi>j</mi></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\gamma_j</annotation></semantics></math></span> in the sense that for any <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>ξ</mi><mo>&gt;</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\xi&gt;1</annotation></semantics></math></span> there is a constant <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>L</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">L</annotation></semantics></math></span> such that \[\mathbb P \Big (\exists \, j : \; |\lambda_j-\gamma_j| \ge (\log N)^L \Big [ \min \big (\, j, N-j+1 \, \big) \Big ]^{-1/3} N^{-2/3} \Big) \le C\exp{\big[-c(\log N)^{\xi} \big]} \] for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span> large enough. (2) The proof of the {\it Dyson's conjecture} \cite{Dy} which states that the time scale of the Dyson Brownian motion to reach local equilibrium is of order <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>N</mi><mrow><mo>−</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N^{-1}</annotation></semantics></math></span>. (3) The edge universality holds in the sense that the probability distributions of the largest (and the smallest) eigenvalues of two generalized Wigner ensembles are the same in the large <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span> limit provided that the second moments of the two ensembles are identical.</p>{% endraw %}
