{% raw %}<p>A finite density of mutually avoiding directed polymers in a quenched random medium is a minimal model of glassy line matter. The dilute theory, solved by replica Bethe ansatz, predicts an interaction free energy proportional to <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>ρ</mi><mn>2</mn></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\rho^2</annotation></semantics></math></span> and disorder cumulants with distinct power-law dependences on the density <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>ρ</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\rho</annotation></semantics></math></span>, but direct numerical tests have been hindered by the combinatorially large many-polymer transfer matrix. We recast the problem as filling logarithmic eigenvalues of a single-polymer transfer-matrix product, obtaining the quenched free energy, its cumulants, and a disorder-induced linear spectral edge consistent with the replica prediction.</p>{% endraw %}
