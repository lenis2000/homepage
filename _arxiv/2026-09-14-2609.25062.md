{% raw %}<p>We study the minimum and maximum expected scores over all adaptive strategies in sequential card guessing with yes/no feedback. A deck contains <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n</annotation></semantics></math></span> labels, each appearing <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>m</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">m</annotation></semantics></math></span> times, and is shuffled uniformly. The cards are guessed one at a time, and after each guess the player is told only whether it was correct. Always guessing the same label gives exactly <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>m</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">m</annotation></semantics></math></span> points. How far can the expected score depart from <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>m</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">m</annotation></semantics></math></span> if all previous feedback is used either to seek or to avoid correct guesses? Diaconis, Graham, and Spiro conjectured that the minimum expectation is <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>m</mi><mo>−</mo><mi>o</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>m</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">m-o(m)</annotation></semantics></math></span> as <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>m</mi><mo>→</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">∞</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">m\to\infty</annotation></semantics></math></span> with <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n</annotation></semantics></math></span> sufficiently large in terms of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>m</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">m</annotation></semantics></math></span>, and that, for each fixed <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>m</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">m</annotation></semantics></math></span>, the limit superior of the maximum expectation as <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi><mo>→</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">∞</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n\to\infty</annotation></semantics></math></span> is at most <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>e</mi><mo>−</mo><mn>1</mn><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mi>m</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">(e-1)m</annotation></semantics></math></span>. Writing these extrema as <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msubsup><mi>P</mi><mrow><mi>m</mi><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi>n</mi></mrow><mo>−</mo></msubsup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">P^-_{m,n}</annotation></semantics></math></span> and <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msubsup><mi>P</mi><mrow><mi>m</mi><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi>n</mi></mrow><mo>+</mo></msubsup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">P^+_{m,n}</annotation></semantics></math></span>, we prove \[ 0\le m-P^-_{m,n}\le3m^{3/4} \qquad(n\ge m\ge1), \] and the finite-parameter bound \[ \frac{P^+_{m,n}}m\le\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac1{k!}<e-1 \] for all positive integers <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>m</mi><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi>n</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">m,n</annotation></semantics></math></span>. The first bound gives a first-order asymptotic uniform in <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi><mo>≥</mo><mi>m</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n\ge m</annotation></semantics></math></span>; the universal constant <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>e</mi><mo>−</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">e-1</annotation></semantics></math></span> in the second cannot be reduced. Thus both conjectures follow. The lower bound combines a switching comparison for restricted permutations with martingale second-moment estimates. For the upper bound, we represent the repeated cards as a random interleaving of independent decks of distinct cards and prove that the optimal expectation is additive when the component-deck labels are revealed.</p>{% endraw %}
