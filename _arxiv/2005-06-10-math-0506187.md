{% raw %}<p>Yor's generalized meander is a temporally inhomogeneous modification of the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>2</mn><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>ν</mi><mo>+</mo><mn>1</mn><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">2(\nu+1)</annotation></semantics></math></span>-dimensional Bessel process with <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>ν</mi><mo>&gt;</mo><mo>−</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\nu &gt; -1</annotation></semantics></math></span>, in which the inhomogeneity is indexed by <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>κ</mi><mo>∈</mo><mo stretchy="false">[</mo><mn>0</mn><mo separator="true">,</mo><mn>2</mn><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>ν</mi><mo>+</mo><mn>1</mn><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\kappa \in [0, 2(\nu+1))</annotation></semantics></math></span>. We introduce the non-colliding particle systems of the generalized meanders and prove that they are the Pfaffian processes, in the sense that any multitime correlation function is given by a Pfaffian. In the infinite particle limit, we show that the elements of matrix kernels of the obtained infinite Pfaffian processes are generally expressed by the Riemann-Liouville differintegrals of functions comprising the Bessel functions <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>J</mi><mi>ν</mi></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">J_{\nu}</annotation></semantics></math></span> used in the fractional calculus, where orders of differintegration are determined by <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>ν</mi><mo>−</mo><mi>κ</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\nu-\kappa</annotation></semantics></math></span>. As special cases of the two parameters <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>ν</mi><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi>κ</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">(\nu, \kappa)</annotation></semantics></math></span>, the present infinite systems include the quaternion determinantal processes studied by Forrester, Nagao and Honner and by Nagao, which exhibit the temporal transitions between the universality classes of random matrix theory.</p>{% endraw %}
