Let <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>M</mi><mi>n</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>α</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">M_n(\a)</annotation></semantics></math></span> denote the <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi><mo>×</mo><mi>n</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n \times n</annotation></semantics></math></span> random matrix whose entries are iid copies of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>α</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\a</annotation></semantics></math></span> and <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>σ</mi><mi>n</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msub><mi>M</mi><mi>n</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>α</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\sigma_n(M_n(\a))</annotation></semantics></math></span> denote the least singular value of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>M</mi><mi>n</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>α</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">M_n(\a)</annotation></semantics></math></span>. (<span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>σ</mi><mi>n</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msub><mi>M</mi><mi>n</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>α</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><msup><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mn>2</mn></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\sigma_n(M_n(\a))^2</annotation></semantics></math></span> is also usually interpreted as the least eigenvalue of the Wishart matrix <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>M</mi><mi>n</mi></msub><msubsup><mi>M</mi><mi>n</mi><mo lspace="0em" rspace="0em">∗</mo></msubsup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">M_n M_n^{\ast}</annotation></semantics></math></span>.) We show that (under a finite moment assumption) the probability distribution <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi><msub><mi>σ</mi><mi>n</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msub><mi>M</mi><mi>n</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>α</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><msup><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mn>2</mn></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n \sigma_n(M_n(\a))^2</annotation></semantics></math></span> is {\it universal} in the sense that it does not depend on the distribution of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>α</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\a</annotation></semantics></math></span>. In particular, it converges to the same limiting distribution as in the special case when <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>a</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">a</annotation></semantics></math></span> is real gaussian. (The limiting distribution was computed explicitly in this case by Edelman.) We also proved a similar result for complex-valued random variables of mean zero, with real and imaginary parts having variance 1/2 and covariance zero. Similar results are also obtained for the joint distribution of the bottom <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>k</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">k</annotation></semantics></math></span> singular values of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>M</mi><mi>n</mi></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>α</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">M_n(\a)</annotation></semantics></math></span> for any fixed <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>k</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">k</annotation></semantics></math></span> (or even for <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>k</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">k</annotation></semantics></math></span> growing as a small power of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n</annotation></semantics></math></span>) and for rectangular matrices. Our approach is motivated by the general idea of "property testing" from combinatorics and theoretical computer science. This seems to be a new approach in the study of spectra of random matrices and combines tools from various areas of mathematics.</p>{% endraw %}
