{% raw %}<p>We study the phenomenon of "crowding" near the largest eigenvalue <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>λ</mi><mi>max</mi><mo>⁡</mo></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\lambda_{\max}</annotation></semantics></math></span> of random <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi><mo>×</mo><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N \times N</annotation></semantics></math></span> matrices belonging to the Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE) of random matrix theory. We focus on two distinct quantities: (i) the density of states (DOS) near <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>λ</mi><mi>max</mi><mo>⁡</mo></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\lambda_{\max}</annotation></semantics></math></span>, <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>ρ</mi><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">D</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">O</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">S</mi></mrow></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>r</mi><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi>N</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\rho_{\rm DOS}(r,N)</annotation></semantics></math></span>, which is the average density of eigenvalues located at a distance <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>r</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">r</annotation></semantics></math></span> from <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>λ</mi><mi>max</mi><mo>⁡</mo></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\lambda_{\max}</annotation></semantics></math></span> and (ii) the probability density function of the gap between the first two largest eigenvalues, <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>p</mi><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">G</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">A</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">P</mi></mrow></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>r</mi><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi>N</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">p_{\rm GAP}(r,N)</annotation></semantics></math></span>. In the edge scaling limit where <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>r</mi><mo>=</mo><mi mathvariant="script">O</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msup><mi>N</mi><mrow><mo>−</mo><mn>1</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mn>6</mn></mrow></msup><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">r = {\cal O}(N^{-1/6})</annotation></semantics></math></span>, which is described by a double scaling limit of a system of unconventional orthogonal polynomials, we show that <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>ρ</mi><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">D</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">O</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">S</mi></mrow></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>r</mi><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi>N</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\rho_{\rm DOS}(r,N)</annotation></semantics></math></span> and <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>p</mi><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">G</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">A</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">P</mi></mrow></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>r</mi><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi>N</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">p_{\rm GAP}(r,N)</annotation></semantics></math></span> are characterized by scaling functions which can be expressed in terms of the solution of a Lax pair associated to the Painlev\'e XXXIV equation. This provides an alternative and simpler expression for the gap distribution, which was recently studied by Witte, Bornemann and Forrester in Nonlinearity 26, 1799 (2013). Our expressions allow to obtain precise asymptotic behaviors of these scaling functions both for small and large arguments.</p>{% endraw %}
