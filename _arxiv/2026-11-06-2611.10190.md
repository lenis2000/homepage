{% raw %}<p>We provide a geometric construction of the radial Dunkl and Heckman-Opdam processes for arbitrary root systems and arbitrary nonnegative real root multiplicity parameters. This is a broad class of processes including the classical Bessel process, Dyson Brownian motion, and the Dyson Bessel process. We find that, when <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>x</mi><mi>t</mi></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">x_t</annotation></semantics></math></span> is a certain anisotropic Markov diffusion process in a symmetric space <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>G</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mi>K</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">G/K</annotation></semantics></math></span> or its tangent space at the origin, invariant under an appropriate <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>K</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">K</annotation></semantics></math></span>-action, the evolution in the space of orbits can be described as a radial Dunkl or Heckman-Opdam process (respective to whether the diffusion is on the tangent space or <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>G</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mi>K</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">G/K</annotation></semantics></math></span> itself). These processes can have arbitrary nonnegative real root multiplicities, analogous to arbitrary values of <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta</annotation></semantics></math></span> in Dyson Brownian motion. In a freezing limit, analogous to <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi><mo>→</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">∞</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta \to \infty</annotation></semantics></math></span> for Dyson Brownian motion, these processes become motion by mean curvature of the orbits. A connection to finite free probability is also discussed, in which we provide a geometric interpretation of certain properties related to finite free position.</p>{% endraw %}
