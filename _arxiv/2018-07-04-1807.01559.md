In the bulk of the spectrum and in the large <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span> limit, we obtain the following results. (i) The semicircle law holds up to the scale <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>N</mi><mrow><mo>−</mo><mn>1</mn><mo>+</mo><mi>ε</mi></mrow></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N^{-1+\varepsilon}</annotation></semantics></math></span> for any <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>ε</mi><mo>&gt;</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\varepsilon&gt;0</annotation></semantics></math></span>. (ii) The eigenvalues locally converge to the point process given by the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble at any fixed energy. (iii) All eigenvectors are delocalized, meaning their <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi mathvariant="normal">L</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">∞</mi></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\rm L}^\infty</annotation></semantics></math></span> norms are all simultaneously bounded by <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>N</mi><mrow><mo>−</mo><mfrac><mn>1</mn><mn>2</mn></mfrac><mo>+</mo><mi>ε</mi></mrow></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}</annotation></semantics></math></span> (after normalization in <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi mathvariant="normal">L</mi><mn>2</mn></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\rm L}^2</annotation></semantics></math></span>) with overwhelming probability, for any <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>ε</mi><mo>&gt;</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\varepsilon&gt;0</annotation></semantics></math></span>. (iv )Quantum unique ergodicity holds, in the sense that the local <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi mathvariant="normal">L</mi><mn>2</mn></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\rm L}^2</annotation></semantics></math></span> mass of eigenvectors becomes equidistributed with overwhelming probability. We extend the mean-field reduction method \cite{BouErdYauYin2017}, which required <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>W</mi><mo>=</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">Ω</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>N</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">W=\Omega(N)</annotation></semantics></math></span>, to the current setting <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>W</mi><mo>≥</mo><msup><mi>N</mi><mrow><mn>3</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mn>4</mn><mo>+</mo><mi>ε</mi></mrow></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">W \ge N^{3/4+\varepsilon}</annotation></semantics></math></span>. Two new ideas are: (1) A new estimate on the "generalized resolvent" of band matrices when <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>W</mi><mo>≥</mo><msup><mi>N</mi><mrow><mn>3</mn><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mn>4</mn><mo>+</mo><mi>ε</mi></mrow></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">W \geq N^{3/4+\varepsilon}</annotation></semantics></math></span>. Its proof, along with an improved fluctuation average estimate, will be presented in parts 2 and 3 of this series \cite {BouYanYauYin2018,YanYin2018}. (2) A strong (high probability) version of the quantum unique ergodicity property of random matrices. For its proof, we construct perfect matching observables of eigenvector overlaps and show they satisfying the eigenvector moment flow equation \cite{BouYau2017} under the matrix Brownian motions.</p>{% endraw %}
