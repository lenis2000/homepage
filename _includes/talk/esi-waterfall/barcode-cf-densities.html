One Particle per Two Sites, and the Imbalance

Theorem (filling) [Knizel–P., in preparation]
The diagonal \(d_a:=\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{barcode}}(a,a)\), given by the paired series, satisfies
\(d_a+d_{a+1}=1,\qquad a\in\mathbb{Z}.\)
Exactly one particle per two-site cell: the slope \(\frac12\) of the waterfall.
Theorem (imbalance)
\(I:=d_0-d_1=\rho(q)\,\mathrm{Pc}(w)\), with \(\rho(q)\) independent of \(w\), where
\(\displaystyle \mathrm{Pc}(w)=-\frac{(q^2;q^2)_\infty^2}{(q;q)_\infty^4}\,\frac{\theta_q(w)}{\theta_q(-w)}\)
is elliptic on \(E=\mathbb{C}^\times/q^{2\mathbb{Z}}\): \(\mathrm{Pc}(qw)=\mathrm{Pc}(1/w)=-\mathrm{Pc}(w)\); zeros \(q^{\mathbb{Z}}\) (there \(d_0=d_1=\frac12\)), poles \(-q^{\mathbb{Z}}\).
Numerically \(\rho(q)=1\) (to 40+ digits), so \(d_{0,1}=\frac12\bigl(1\pm\mathrm{Pc}(w)\bigr)\). Proof in progress.
