Glauber Dynamics and Coupling From the Past

Glauber dynamics: add or remove one cube (rotate three lozenges around a vertex), accepted with probability \(\min\bigl(1,\mathrm{w}(h\pm1)/\mathrm{w}(h)\bigr)\). Reversible for \(\prod \mathrm{w}(h)\); only approximate samples. Uniform hexagon: mixing conjecturally in \(N^{4+o(1)}\) flips; proved without frozen regions [Laslier–Toninelli 2015]
Coupling from the past [Propp–Wilson 1996]: the dynamics is monotone in the height function, so it suffices to run the maximal and minimal tilings with common randomness from times \(-1,-2,-4,\ldots\). Coalescence by time \(0\) ⇒ exact sample
Fails for the waterfall: to coalesce, horizontal lozenges must cross the waterfall region, where they are exponentially rare [Knizel–P. 2025]. The sandwich does not close in any reasonable time.
