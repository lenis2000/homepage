Why the Paths Cluster: Exponential Concentration

Heuristics. A hole (horizontal lozenge) at \((t,x)\) carries the weight
\(\displaystyle \mathrm{w}\bigl(x-\tfrac{t}{2}+1\bigr) = \chi\, q^{\frac12 + \left(x - \frac{t+S}{2}\right)} + \frac{1}{\chi\, q^{\frac12 + \left(x - \frac{t+S}{2}\right)}}\)
Both terms are \(O(1)\) near the center line \(x = \tfrac{t+S}{2}\), and one of them is exponentially large in \(L\) far from it \(\Rightarrow\) holes are pushed away from the center line, symmetrically. If \(\mathsf{t}_l < \mathsf{t} < \mathsf{t}_r\), there is room for a band of width \(\mathsf{N}\) of particles; otherwise the paths are frozen with slope \(0\) or \(1\).
Proof idea (on a vertical slice, via the \(q\)-Racah orthogonal polynomial ensemble):
  • Compare two configurations differing by moving one particle \(x \to y\): the ratio of probabilities has a dominant factor \(q^{\mathscr{E}(x,y)}\)
  • \(\mathscr{E}\) = one-body term (integrated from \(x\) to \(y\)) + interaction with the other \(N-1\) particles
  • The interaction is minimized when the other particles form a densely packed block around the center line
  • Moving a particle toward the center line gains \(q^{-cL}\) near the boundary of \(\mathcal{W}\), and \(q^{-c'L^2}\) across it
Theorem [Knizel-P. 2025] For any \(\varepsilon > 0\), there is \(c > 0\) with
\(\displaystyle \mathbb{P}\Bigl(\exists \text{ hole at } (t,y)\colon\ \mathsf{t}_l + \varepsilon < \tfrac{t}{L} < \mathsf{t}_r - \varepsilon,\ \ \Bigl|\tfrac{y}{L} - \tfrac{\mathsf{S}+\mathsf{t}}{2}\Bigr| < \tfrac{\mathsf{N}-\varepsilon}{2}\Bigr) < e^{-cL}\)
