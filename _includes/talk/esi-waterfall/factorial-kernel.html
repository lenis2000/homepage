Kernel and Residue Localization [Li, in prep.]

Theorem [Li, in prep.] The (ascending) factorial Schur process is determinantal, with kernel
\[K(t_1,z_1;t_2,z_2) = \frac{1}{(2\pi \mathrm{i})^2} \oint\!\!\oint \frac{du\,dv}{v-u} \prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{v-x_i}{u-x_i}\; \frac{\prod_{j=1}^{t_2}(w_j-u)\,\prod_{k=1}^{z_2-1}(u+y_k)}{\prod_{j=1}^{t_1}(w_j-v)\,\prod_{k=1}^{z_1}(v+y_k)}\]
Schur-type structure (Cauchy identity + Eynard-Mehta) ⇒ asymptotics accessible in the bulk, at the edge, and at the corner
Steepest descent fails
  • \(q\) fixed: all \(q\)-Pochhammer factors converge; what remains of the action is \(S(v)=K\log(v-1)\), which has no saddle point
  • Poles form geometric strings \(-\alpha q^{-\ell}\), \(\beta q^{-\ell}\) of length \(\asymp M\); the contours are trapped between them
Discrete residue localization
  • Saddle equation → logarithmic balance
    \(2\rho-2+t+z-K=0\) picks a center \(R_M=\rho M\) on the pole string
  • Kernel = sum of residues, recentered \(\ell = R-R_M\)
  • Gaussian decay → \(|\mathrm{Res}_\ell| \le C\,q^{\ell^2/2-c|\ell|}\), then dominated convergence
